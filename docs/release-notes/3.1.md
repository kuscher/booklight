Booklight 3.1: first steps. A welcome at the very first opening, a key of your own in a minute, three things to
try, and a way to see it all again.

- **A welcome, once.** A new installation's very first opening is a slow one: the glass grows tall and goes to
  night, a lamp strikes at the caret, and "Welcome to Booklight" stands in its light with a small Swiss Army
  knife. Then Booklight shows off with its own panel: your apps, a sum, an example flight, the emoji grid. It runs
  nothing and sends nothing. Enter or Space begins the show; Esc, or simply typing, ends it.
- **Give Booklight a key.** Action + Quick Insert is suggested (Action + M on a keyboard without that key). "Open
  Keyboard shortcuts" opens the system's dialog on a page of Booklight's own with the five steps, and the panel
  waits behind it. Press your new keys: the dialog goes, and "Your key works".
- **Three things to try**, each once and for practice: open an app, search inside one, a sum. Nothing opens; the
  footer says what Enter would have done, and the sum is copied.
- **One question**: may searches be suggested as you type? Nothing is chosen for you, "Not now" comes first, and
  only "Agree" switches suggestions on. Then two choices: "Show your usual", and the list of everything.
- **It never nags.** Esc leaves at any point. An unfinished run stands in three openings and then waits in the
  Booklight window; "Not now" on the key ends it.
- **First steps, again.** Type `first steps` in the panel, or choose First steps on the window's Start page,
  which plays the welcome too. Where the steps were left unfinished, both go on where they stopped.
- **The screens are set down, not shown**: caps come up a beat apart, a recipe is written a letter at a time, an
  answer shows as pressed, and the key that lands is held down on the glass and comes up with its check. With the
  system's animations off everything simply stands.
- The window's Start page and the README tell the same way to a key.

Updating from 3.0: if Booklight has no key yet, you are shown the key's step once; with a key, nothing changes
until you ask for First steps. No new permission, nothing in the background, no new connection.
