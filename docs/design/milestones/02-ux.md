# Booklight after 2.1: the four milestones as interaction

*UX designer's paper, 1 October 2026. Planning only: nothing was built, no device was touched, nothing in the
repo was changed. It works through the PM's frame (`01-frame.md`) and was checked against the engineer's paper
(`03-eng.md`). Read for it: `docs/design/ux-model.md` (with §14), `docs/design/reviews-2.0/ux.md` (the table of
keys), `docs/design/design-system.md`, the five research files, and the code where it says what exists today:
`scopes/Picks.kt`, `scopes/Prompts.kt`, `scopes/Scopes.kt`, `overlay/OverlayModel.kt`, `overlay/OverlayActivity.kt`,
`overlay/Metrics.kt`, `overlay/Panel.kt`, and the string resources in English and German.*

*File: `docs/design/milestones/02-ux.md`*

Sources as in the frame: **NF2**, **S&C**, **DF**, **LANG**, **NB**. **ENG** = `03-eng.md`. "Today" = 2.1 as released.
"Check n" = the engineer's device session (ENG §12).

**How a screen is written here.** `[chip]` is the chip in the field. "●" is the selected row (it has the pill),
"○" any other row. A row is: its mark, its title, its second line if it has one, its label at the right end. On
the selected row the right end shows its actions: the armed one by name with ⏎, the others as icons, named in
brackets.

## 1. The short version

1. **One answer for the copy, the picture and the selection** (the frame's question 3): the thing you brought
   becomes the chip in the field, and the rows under it are what can be done with it. 2.0 does exactly this for
   text another app hands over. The copy and the picture get there by Tab from one quiet row. The selection is
   there when the panel opens.
2. **A model's row is answered where it stands.** Enter asks, the row grows into the answer, the other rows
   fade, the chip stays the thing. Today the chip turns into the prompt. I recommend changing that (§2.3).
3. **The quiet row** is not selected, has nothing armed and shows a `tab` cap: the tip's rules, reused. Enter
   does nothing. Tab or ↓ opens it. Typing puts it away in the same frame.
4. **Few rows.** After Tab: at most three things found in the text, the translation, and the first three of
   your prompts. Everything else is found by typing its name. Copy clean and Copy as Markdown are icons on the
   link's row, not rows.
5. **`do` needs no keyword.** Under the copy's chip, what you type is the instruction, unless it is a row's
   name. From the empty field, `clip` and the instruction does the same. This removes the `do` and Donnerstag
   question, the "do not disturb" trap, and one keyword to translate six times in M4 (§3.4).
6. **The picture:** three doors, one state. Under its chip there is one row: the picture and the text in it,
   with Copy text, Use text and Pin. What you type is the question; its answer is a row above the picture.
7. **The selection:** the menu's names are fixed when the app is built, so the translation's entry is
   "Translate", not "In German". Replace is armed first. A selection of several paragraphs must be replaceable,
   or the promise fails for a mail.
8. **Languages** bring no new interaction. The rules that keep it so are in §6.
9. Counted keys: §7. Where I differ from the frame: §8. Decisions this paper adds (U1 to U12): §9.

## 2. One model for the copy, the picture and the selection

### 2.1 The same four steps

| Step | The copy (M1) | The picture (M2) | The selection (M3) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. It is offered | One quiet row under the empty field, for a copy of the last two minutes | The same row ("a picture") when it came by the clipboard | Not offered: you asked for Booklight in the other app's menu |
| 2. It becomes the chip | Tab or ↓ on the row. Or `clip`, at any age | Tab or ↓. Shared or chosen in the picker, it arrives as the chip | It arrives as the chip |
| 3. The rows | What is in it, then what the model can do | The picture with its text | As the copy |
| 4. A model's row | Answered where it stands | The answer is a row above the picture | Answered where it stands, Replace armed |
| Back | Backspace on the empty field goes one step back: answer, rows, the empty panel. Esc closes at every step | The same | The same |

### 2.2 The chip

- **Text:** the clip mark (a copy) or the text mark (a selection), then the first words of the text. Today the
  label of handed-over text may be 280 dp wide (`design-system.md` §3b). The field now also takes an
  instruction, so I recommend the ordinary 160 dp, fading at its end.
- **Picture:** a small thumbnail in the mark's place, then "Picture".
- It is never kept. ↑ on an empty field never brings it back, and nothing typed under it is sent for
  suggestions. That is today's rule for handed-over text (`OverlayModel`: `foreign`; `Scopes.forget`).
- The placeholder names what to type, as every scope's does: "What to do with it" under text, "A question
  about this picture" under a picture.

### 2.3 A model's row is answered where it stands

**Today.** Enter on "Fix spelling" in the list of a handed-over text turns the chip into the prompt and types
the whole text into the field (`Picks.kt`: `EnterScope(PromptScope.key(p), one)`). For a copied page that is a
field full of text (ENG §2.1 says the same).

**Recommended.** The chip stays the thing. The row that was asked rides to the first place if it was not
there, grows to the answer row (92 dp, then four lines if the answer needs them), and the other rows fade
where they are. That is what an opened row does to the list today (`reviews-2.0/ux.md` M1). Its caption names
the prompt, because the chip no longer does: "In English", then "In English · on this device".

**Why.** One rule for three things, and the picture needs it anyway: there is no prompt to turn its chip
into. The field stays free for a next instruction. The thing stays in sight.

**If it is too dear for M1:** the engineer's plan (ENG §5.3) is the fallback. The chip turns into the prompt,
the field stays empty, the prompt takes the copy as its material. Then Backspace on the empty field must go
back to the copy's rows, not to the keyword as text. ENG's shared piece 1 ("any scope can have a row answered
by the model") is what makes the recommended form possible.

**What does not change.** A prompt typed by its keyword (`fix teh text`) is its own chip, as today. Under a
thing's chip the model is asked on Enter only, never after a pause in typing: an answer costs a second and an
allowance nobody publishes (S&C §1.4).

### 2.4 Keys in the three new states

| Key | The quiet row (empty field) | A thing's rows | A row being answered, or answered |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Typing | The row goes in the same frame. Ordinary results | Narrows the rows by name. What matches no row is the instruction (text) or the question (picture), as one row | Takes the answer back. The rows return, narrowed |
| Tab | Opens it. First press only | The selected row's next action, wrapping | Next action |
| ↓ | Opens it | Next row | Under a picture: to the picture's row. Else nothing |
| ↑ | The last text, as today. The row goes | Previous row | Nothing |
| Enter | **Nothing** (D4) | Runs the armed action. On a model's row: asks. After the Tab that opened it, only a new press 350 ms later (today's rule for anything that fills the field for you) | While it arrives: waits for all of it, then does the armed action, as today |
| Shift + Enter | Nothing | Runs, stays | Copies, stays |
| → ← | Nothing | Along the row's actions | Along its actions |
| Backspace on the empty field | Nothing | The chip goes. The quiet row is back if the copy is still fresh | The rows are back |
| Ctrl + 1…9 | Nothing | That row's first action. Never asks the model (today: "an answer is Enter's") | Nothing |
| Esc · the key again · a click outside | Closes | Closes | Closes |
| Click | On the row: opens it | A row: its first action | An action: does it |

Footer: the quiet row has none, like a tip. Under a chip it is today's: `tab` Actions, `⌫` Leave while the
field is empty, `esc` Close.

### 2.5 The empty panel's guests (the frame's question 1)

One place under the field, one guest at a time.

| Order | Guest | Shows when | Never with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A first-run card (the key, suggestions) | As today | Anything else. A new user learns the key first |
| 2 | The copy's row | Something was copied in the last two minutes, it is not marked private, it is not Booklight's own copy, the switch is on | A tip. The tip keeps its turn for another opening |
| 3 | A tip | As today, and no fresh copy | The copy's row |
| beside them | The white reflection on the edge (today's polish) | Once, a few seconds after opening, on a panel at rest | Anything arriving or leaving under the field, and the edge light of a model that is thinking |

- The row arrives when a tip does: after the glass has opened, never as part of the opening. A panel opened
  with something already in it (shared text, a picture, an example typed from the window) gets no row.
- Its words may settle once. Right after Ctrl + C the system may still be looking at the text (DF found it done
  after two seconds; sooner is not verified, check 3). The row then says "text" and its words roll once, in
  place, when the system has finished. Its height and place never change (ENG §5.1 builds it this way).
- The reflection must not start in the frame the row arrives or its words settle. The working tree has it at
  2.4 s after opening and 0.7 s of quiet today; the copy's row counts as "not quiet" while it moves.
- Once the model has been asked in this opening, the reflection does not come: the edge then belongs to the
  model's lap.
- Whether the row is too old is decided once, when the panel opens. It does not vanish while you look at it.
- The field emptied again brings the row back after the same short wait (U12). Backspace out of its chip does
  the same.

### 2.6 When the model does not answer, or is wrong (one state for every row)

The state is today's (`Prompts.kt`: `unanswered`): the caption says it, the thing's first line stays, the way
to the Gemini app is the action. No colour, no warning mark. The engineer keeps the reason (ENG piece 1); these
are the words.

| What happened | Caption, English | Caption, German | Actions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| This device has no model | The row's label is "Gemini" from the start, not "On this device" | "Gemini" | Ask Gemini ⏎: the Gemini app opens with the prompt and the text, not sent (today) |
| The system can fetch one | A last row, "Get the on-device model" (today) | „Modell für dieses Gerät laden“ | Get |
| Too many questions in a short time | The model is busy. Try again in a moment | Das Modell ist beschäftigt. Gleich noch einmal versuchen | Ask ⏎ · Ask Gemini |
| The allowance is used up | No more answers on this device for now | Vorerst keine Antworten mehr auf diesem Gerät | Ask Gemini ⏎ |
| It will not answer this text | The model on this device does not answer this | Das Modell auf diesem Gerät beantwortet das nicht | Ask Gemini ⏎ |
| It said nothing in time, or anything else | No answer on this device this time (today) | Diesmal keine Antwort auf diesem Gerät | Ask Gemini ⏎ |
| The answer was cut | "… · cut off here" after the caption | „… · hier abgeschnitten“ | Copy ⏎ · Pin · Open in Gemini. **Never Replace first** |
| The text is too long to rewrite here (ENG: over about 3,000 characters) | The row's label is "Gemini" before Enter | "Gemini" | Ask Gemini ⏎ |
| The text is too long to read whole (Summary, Explain, an instruction) | From the first 1,200 of 3,400 words | Aus den ersten 1.200 von 3.400 Wörtern | as any answer |

A picture has no way to the Gemini app until someone has checked that the app takes one (ENG §6.2: not
verified).

## 3. M1 · The copy

### 3.1 What the quiet row says (the frame's question 4)

It can never show the text: it has not read it. The system's description gives the kind, the age and what the
system found, as scores per kind, without their places (S&C §4.1).

| The description says | English | German |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A link | Copied: a link | Kopiert: ein Link |
| A date or a time | Copied: a date | Kopiert: ein Datum |
| A phone number · an address · a mail address · a flight number | a phone number · an address · an email address · a flight | eine Telefonnummer · eine Adresse · eine E-Mail-Adresse · ein Flug |
| Two things | Copied: a link and a date | Kopiert: ein Link und ein Datum |
| Three or more | Copied: a link, a date and more | Kopiert: ein Link, ein Datum und mehr |
| Text with nothing found, or not looked at yet | Copied: text | Kopiert: Text |
| Text the system did not look at (over 400 characters) | Copied: a longer text | Kopiert: ein längerer Text |
| A picture (M2) | Copied: a picture | Kopiert: ein Bild |
| Marked private · Booklight's own copy · older than two minutes · a file or anything else | No row | |

- The kinds are always named in one order: link, date, phone number, address, mail address, flight. The rows
  after Tab keep that order, so the row and the list agree.
- "A longer text" only if check 3 shows that "not looked at" always means "too long" on a Googlebook.
  Otherwise "text".
- Right end: the age in third ink, then a `tab` cap. "just now" under ten seconds, then "20 s ago" in steps
  of ten, then "1 min ago". German: „gerade eben“, „vor 20 s“, „vor 1 min“. It does not tick.
- No pill, no footer, no kind label.

### 3.2 Flows

**A. A text you cannot read, to a translation on the clipboard.** *Before:* a mail in German in Gmail,
Booklight in English. You select a paragraph and press Ctrl + C.

1. **The key.** The panel opens at the field's height. Field: empty, "Search apps, settings and the web".
   When the glass is open the row comes: clip mark · "Copied: text" · "just now" · `tab`.
2. **Tab.** The system shows its "pasted" message, once. Field: `[clip · Am Freitag treffen wir…]`, "What to
   do with it".
   - ● spark · In English · Ask ⏎
   - ○ spark · Fix spelling · On this device
   - ○ spark · Shorter · On this device
   - Footer: `⌫` Leave · `esc` Close
3. **Enter.** The row grows. Caption "In English", under it the first line of the German text, dim; the edge
   light runs its slow lap. Then the answer is written in and the caption reads "In English · on this device".
   Actions: Copy ⏎ · (Pin) · (Open in Gemini). The other rows have faded.
4. **Enter.** Pressed early, it waits for the whole answer. Footer: ✓ Copied. The panel closes.

*After:* the keys are back in Gmail. Ctrl + V pastes the translation. Four presses, no typing.

**B. A line with a link and a date.** *Before:* a chat message, "Dinner with Anna on Friday at 7pm. Call
+49 30 5550 1234 or book at https://example.com/table?utm_source=chat". Ctrl + C.

1. **The key.** Row: "Copied: a link, a date and more" · "just now" · `tab`.
2. **Tab.** Field: `[clip · Dinner with Anna on…]`. The rows are in §3.7, screen 2.
3. **Enter** opens the link in the browser. Or **Tab, Enter**: Copy clean, footer ✓ Copied. Or **↓, Enter**:
   the Event row as today, filled in (When "Fri 2 Oct, 19:00–20:00", Title "Dinner with Anna"); **Enter**
   again opens the calendar's editor. Nothing is saved until you save it there.

*After:* the browser, the clipboard, or the calendar's editor.

**C. Say what to do with it.** *Before:* the notes of a meeting, copied.

1. **The key.** "Copied: a longer text". **Tab.** Fix spelling · Shorter · In German · Summary.
2. **Type** `pull out the tasks as a list`. No row has that name, so one row is left, already tall:
   ● spark · caption "With what you copied" · the first two lines of the notes, dim · Ask ⏎.
3. **Enter.** The answer is written in. Caption: "On this device", or "On this device · from the first 1,200
   of 3,400 words" when the notes did not fit. Actions: Copy ⏎ · (Pin) · (Open in Gemini).
4. **Enter** copies. Or **Tab, Enter** pins the list on top of your windows.

Without Tab: `clip pull out the tasks as a list` from the empty field is the same from step 2.

### 3.3 The rows after Tab

| Row | Title | Second line | Label at rest | Selected | Enter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A link | The address without `https://` | none | Link | Open ⏎ · (Copy clean) · (Copy as Markdown) | Opens it in the browser |
| A date | The day and time as Booklight's own reader understood them | The title the event would get | Event | Add… ⏎ | The Event row, filled in; Enter there opens the calendar's editor |
| A phone number | The number | none | Phone | Call ⏎ · (Copy), or Copy ⏎ alone | Call only if an app answers it. **Not verified** (check 7) |
| An address | The address | none | Address | Open in Maps ⏎ · (Copy) | A Maps address in the browser. Addresses were not found in the probe (DF) |
| A mail address | The address | none | Mail | Compose ⏎ · (Copy) | The compose window, as `mail` |
| A flight | "LH 454" | none | Flight | Look up ⏎ | A web search for the number. Booklight can do no more (ENG §3) |
| The translation | "In English" or "In German" | none | On this device | Ask ⏎ | Answered where it stands |
| A prompt of yours | Its name | none | On this device | Ask ⏎ | Answered where it stands |
| What you typed, when no row has that name | As row one: tall, caption "With what you copied". Lower: "Do this with it" | The copy's first lines | On this device | Ask ⏎ | Answered where it stands |

**The order.**
1. The translation, **if the text is not in your language**. You may not be able to read the rest.
2. What is in it: three rows at most, in the fixed order of §3.1.
3. Your prompts, the first three in the order they have under Yours. The ready-made translation is one of
   them. It turns round by itself: a text in your language goes into your other language (German in an English
   Booklight, English otherwise: ENG §5.3), any other comes into yours.
4. Summary as well, for a long text.

That is three to seven rows. Eight fit. Everything else is found by typing its name: Explain, your other
prompts, and what `clip` lists today (Note, Mail, QR code, UPPERCASE, lowercase, Title Case, On one line,
Counted, URL-encoded, Search, Ask Gemini). The rule is today's for handed-over text: three prompts while
nothing is typed, "a name finds the rest" (`Picks.kt`).

**Three small rules.**
- Copy clean shows only on a link that has a tail to take off.
- The prompt rows carry no second line. Today each repeats the text; the chip shows it now.
- The list arrives whole. Nothing is put in above the selected row afterwards (ENG §5.2 agrees: what cannot
  be found in about 150 ms is left out).

### 3.4 "Say what to do with it" (the frame's question 5)

- **Where the instruction shows:** in the field, after the chip. **Where the material shows:** in the chip
  (its first words) and in the row before the answer (its first lines, dim, under "With what you copied").
- **No keyword of its own (U2).** The research's `do` is a common first word. "do not disturb" is a settings
  page, and `do` and a space would swallow it (ENG §5.4 found the same). "do I need a visa" is a question.
  `clip` is already the keyword for what you copied, and nobody searches for it. So `clip` and an instruction
  is the typed form, and Tab on the row is the short one.
- **If you want the word:** English `do`, German `mach` (`do` stays, as English keywords do). Then it becomes
  the chip by Tab only, until you have used it once. That is how another app's keyword behaves today
  (`spaceEnters`). And a settings page that matches the whole text comes first. `do` as Donnerstag is then no
  matter: the date reader only sees it inside `termin` and `erinnere`.
- **Nothing copied:** one row with nothing to run: "The clipboard is empty" (today's words).
- **A copy marked private:** one row with nothing to run: "What you copied is marked private". Booklight does
  not read it. Today `clip` does read such a copy (`Picks.kt`: `clipboardText`). I recommend that stops (U6).
- **Too long:** §2.6. A rewrite is never cut silently: half a translation that looks whole is worse than
  none. A summary or an instruction reads what fits and says how much that was.

### 3.5 When it is not the happy path

| Case | What the user sees |
| --- | --- |
| Nothing copied, or older than two minutes | No row. A tip may come. `clip` still opens an older copy |
| Marked private (a password manager) | No row. `clip`: "What you copied is marked private" |
| Booklight's own copy (an emoji, an answer) | No row |
| The key pressed within a second of Ctrl + C | "Copied: text". The words may settle once (§2.5) |
| A very long text | "a longer text" or "text". After Tab: Summary is added; rows that would rewrite it carry "Gemini" |
| No model on this device (the HP: not verified, check 1) | The row, the link, the date, the number, Copy clean all work. The model's rows carry "Gemini" and hand over |
| The model refuses, is busy, is over its allowance | §2.6 |
| The model translates the wrong way (a short text, the language guessed wrong) | The row names its direction before Enter: "In English". The other way: type `german` and its row comes |
| The date reader cannot read the date the system found | The Event row shows its guess, dim, as today (ENG §5.2). You see the day before the calendar opens |
| A date in a language Booklight is not set to | See §8, point 6 |
| No network | M1 needs none. Open and Ask Gemini start another app, which says so itself |
| German | „Kopiert: ein Link und ein Datum“ · „vor 20 s“. Rows: „Auf Englisch“, „Korrigieren“, „Kürzer“. Placeholder: „Was damit geschehen soll“ |
| The switch is off, or D1 is "no" | No row. Tab on the empty field opens the copy; the rest is the same |

### 3.6 How it fits, and what it breaks

**Reused.** The tip's place, its timing and its rules (nothing armed, Tab, gone on the first letter). The
chip and rows of handed-over text. The answer row, its growing, the lap on the edge, Copy · Pin · Open in
Gemini. The Event row and the date reader. Link rows. "The clipboard is empty". "Get the on-device model".

**New, and why it cannot be an existing piece.**
- *The quiet row.* Not a result: a result is selected and Enter runs it. Not a tip: a tip types an example,
  this opens a thing. It can share the tip card's measure; that is the visual designer's call.
- *A model's row answered under a thing's chip* (§2.3).
- *The row for what you typed.* It is the "Ask Gemini" row's pattern: tall as row one, an ordinary row lower
  down (`design-system.md` §3g).
- *One switch in the window:* "What you copied".

**Rules it bends.**

| Rule | What happens to it | Recommended |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Nothing typed, nothing shown" (PLAN §11.3) | A second exception after tips, and the first that depends on another app | Yes (D1), with the switch. Enter on the empty panel still does nothing |
| Today Enter on a prompt's row turns the chip into the prompt | Changed for all three things | §2.3 |
| `clip` lists eleven rows today | Fewer by default; the others by name | Yes. He found nine icons "too much" |
| `clip` reads a copy marked private | It no longer does | Yes |
| "The order is fixed" (`ux-model.md` §2) | One exception: the translation goes first for a text not in your language | Yes. It is what makes "four presses, no typing" true |

**For the motion designer.** The row arrives as a tip does. On Tab its mark and words should go to the
chip's place, not vanish: things open from where they are. Typing takes the row away in the same frame.
Backspace out of the chip is the same road backwards.

## 4. M2 · The picture

### 4.1 Three doors, one state (the frame's question 6)

It is one flow. Every door ends with the picture as the chip.

| Door | What the user does | What Booklight shows first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The clipboard, if a capture is copied (D5: **not verified**) | Captures a region, presses the key, Tab | The quiet row: picture mark · "Copied: a picture" · "just now" · `tab`. No thumbnail yet: it has not been read |
| Share | In the capture's preview: Share, Booklight (**not verified** that the preview has Share: check 10) | The panel opens with the picture as its chip, as shared text does today |
| The picker | Types `pic`, Enter on "Choose a picture…" | The panel folds away, the system's picker opens with the newest first. On a pick the panel comes back with the picture as its chip. On Cancel it stays away (ENG §6.1: the panel cannot stay open behind the picker) |

`pic` is the one new keyword (German: `bild`, and `pic`). "picture", "image", "screenshot" find its row
without being keywords, as "umlaut" finds `abc` today. `pic` with a picture on the clipboard opens that
picture, at any age. `clip` does too: it is "what you copied", whatever it is.

### 4.2 The rows under a picture

**Nothing typed: one row.** The picture and the text in it, as the QR code's row shows a code and its text.

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Left | The picture, whole, never cropped |
| Right, caption | "Picture · 1600 × 900" |
| Right, lines | The text read from it, up to five lines, in second ink. "Reading…" until it is there. "No text in this picture" if there is none |
| Actions | Copy text ⏎ · (Use text) · (Pin) |

- **Copy text** is first, so Enter on a picture does the one thing that cannot be invented. Pressed before
  the text is read, it waits, as Enter waits for an answer today. No text: the footer says "No text in this
  picture" and the panel stays.

  translation, your prompts, an instruction. Backspace on the empty field comes back to the picture.
- **Pin** keeps the picture on top.
- No Save. The picture exists where it came from. NF2 §2.2 lists it; I would leave it out until asked for.

**Something typed: it is the question.** A row for it comes above the picture and is selected. The picture
rides down and stays in sight.

- The typed words `copy`, `text`, `pin` (and their German words) arm that action on the picture, as a typed
  verb arms an app's action today. Anything else is the question.
- The question's row: caption "About this picture", Ask ⏎. Enter asks. The answer is written into it.
  Actions: Copy ⏎ · (Pin). Pin there pins the answer; ↓, then Pin, pins the picture.

### 4.4 A whole screen, and "this may be wrong" (the frame's question 6)

**A picture too large for the model to read** (ENG §6.2 has the rule; its limit is check 13):
- With the recogniser: the question is asked of the picture's text. The caption says so: "On this device ·
  from the picture's text". Nothing else changes.
- Without it (D6 "no"): there is no Ask. The row says "Too large to read here. Capture a part of the screen."

**How "this may be wrong" is said, without a warning on every row.**
1. Where the answer came from stands in the caption, always: "from the picture" or "from the picture's text".
2. The text that was read stands under the answer, in the picture's row. An answer can be checked against it
   at a glance.
3. The thing that cannot be wrong is the first action: Copy text.
4. The answer runs nothing. It can be copied or pinned.
5. It is said once in words, where words are read once: the tip, the line in the list of everything, and the
   window's About text, which already says who answers.
6. *To consider:* a number in the answer that is nowhere in the picture's text is the commonest invention
   (DF: a whole screen "made text up"). The caption could then read "On this device · check the numbers".
   Booklight's own code can make that check. For the engineer to judge.

## 8. Where I differ from the frame

1. **Build the shared mechanism once, in M1, and let the selection have it at once.** The chip, the rows and
   the answer where it stands are one piece. Today's "Booklight" entry in the menu already hands a selection
   over, so M1 can give it the same rows and Replace at no extra design. M3 is then only the names in the
   menu, the letters, `tr` and three prompts. The engineer moves six shared pieces into M1 for his own reasons
   (ENG §4); this is the same move seen from the user's side.
2. **M1 is "one row, one list", not four features.** "Tell it what to do" is what typing does in that list.
   It needs no keyword and no line of its own in the story.
3. **Copy clean and Copy as Markdown are actions of the link's row,** not entries of the list.
4. **The date goes through the Event row first** (the engineer's proposal, ENG §5.2). One Enter more, and you
   see the day and can mend the title before the calendar opens.
5. **The order M2 before M3: I do not ask for a swap, with one condition.** From the user's side M3 is the
   nearer step after M1: the same chip and rows, one new door. M2 brings a new kind of thing. If D5 comes
   back "no" **and** the capture's preview has no Share (checks 9 and 10), the picture's main road is the
   picker, and M2 is no longer "capture, open, ask". Then swap them (D19 allows it).
6. **Dates in a thing you brought need the text's language, not the app's.** After the groundwork the date
   reader knows the app's language and English (D13). A German mail copied into an English Booklight would
   then lose its "Add…" row. For a copy, a selection and a picture's text, the reader should use the word set
   of the language the text is in, whenever Booklight has one. For the PM and the engineer.
7. **M3's promise needs two things the frame does not name:** a selection of several paragraphs can be
   replaced, and Replace is first. Without them "rewrites it where it stands" is true for one line only.
8. **M2's rows are one row.** "Add the event in it" and the rest of M1 are one step away, behind Use text. I
   would not list them under the picture.
9. **D5 now, not "before M2 is designed".** It also decides whether the quiet row ever says "a picture".

I agree with D1 to D4 as recommended, with two additions: first-run cards come before the copy's row, and ↓
opens the row as Tab does. A row under the field invites ↓, and a dead key there would read as broken.
