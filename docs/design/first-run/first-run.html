First run, in the panel

First run begins with a welcome. The glass grows tall and goes dark, the caret comes back as Booklight’s own lamp, and its light falls on a few words and on a small Swiss Army knife. Then Booklight shows off for six seconds in the real panel: the user’s apps, a sum, an example flight, the emoji grid, one highlight travelling through all of it. Then it asks for one thing, a key, and stands under the field, where rows stand. The field is never covered: type at any moment and it is Booklight. Each step shows what to press as large key caps and has the user do it once for real. It is one opening from the first frame to the bare field, and the user’s keys are pressed once.

Nothing of this is built. On this page: the prototype · how the minute goes · the stage · the moments · the shortcut’s options · what you are asked to decide · what it costs

Motion is off: every screen stands as it ends, as it does with the system’s animations off. There is no welcome and no show then: K1 stands in the first frame, with the welcome’s title as the field’s placeholder.

Drawn, not filmed. The welcome is drawn with what the app has: paths, gradients and type in layers, on one clock; the light is one pass that the app would run as a shader, and the soft glow round the lit letters is the one thing that is a blur. The apps, their icons and the Action key’s glyph are stand-ins: on a device the show has that device’s own apps. The flight is an example held in the app, and nothing is fetched. The emoji and the large rounded type are drawn in this computer’s own fonts. The helper is the system’s dialog: Booklight cannot draw in it, and only the five rows are Booklight’s. It is drawn smaller than it is (on the Lenovo Googlebook about 990 by 955 dp; it covers the panel wholly), and its own words stay English in both languages, because their German must be read off a device. The helper’s coming and going, an app’s window arriving and the fade over a panel that comes back are the system’s, drawn here as about a quarter of a second. Every opening runs at Medium, the default. At quarter speed the motion is four times as long and the user’s pauses are not.

The stage: three ways

The card’s own size, 720 × 172

Free. The caps can be no larger than 24 dp, and the question’s German text does not fit: a disclosure would be cut. It reads as a card with a paragraph.

The same width, taller: 252 and 324 Chosen

Free to move; the layout is the work. Caps as tall as a row, every part on the row grid, the whole disclosure. The daily opening stays as it is.

A larger glass that shrinks, 960 × 404

4 to 5 days and a broken rule: the window is exactly the panel. Caps of 96 dp and an ending in which the glass becomes the panel. The helper hides it for the hard part all the same.

The opening uses what the chosen stage leaves free, for its ten seconds only. The welcome is the one place where the glass is a stage: 720 by 468, the most height that is free, in the dark, with the desk behind dimmed by half. Through the show the lower edge goes to 400, 212, 256 and 376 and settles at 252. The last screen, C, is 268.

The moments

The welcome
Night falls in a tall glass. The caret blinks off, and when it comes back it is a lamp: the field is its head, and its light opens on the stage the way the glass itself opened. The words are white where it falls. A small knife flicks out five tools. Then Booklight presses the handle, the light goes back up into the caret, and the handle rises to be the highlight.
The show
Five of the user’s own apps in a cascade, the highlight down the list and along a row, a sum rolled into its answer, a flight with its plane arriving, seventy emoji in a wave, one lap of light. As the light turns onto the lower edge the highlight glides into “Open Keyboard shortcuts”. Every part is the product at work, and the first key makes it the user’s.
The key caps
As tall as a row, flat, with the white ring, their labels in the field’s type. Typed parts stand beside them on the same baseline.
The reveal
The user’s own keys make the system’s dialog fall away. The key on the glass is down because theirs is. It comes up, the check draws, one lap of light.
The step aside
Enter opens the app. The glass folds to its seam, the app is there, and the seam comes again in the same place: Booklight opens over the app, on the next lesson.
The fold
The last list closes to the bare field: one line of placeholder, one lap of light. First run ends as the product.

The shortcut: what is possible

Android 17 gives an app no way to set a global shortcut or to ask for one, and the system’s Keyboard shortcuts dialog cannot be opened on a chosen page. Every real route goes through that dialog. What can change is the path through it and the guidance, and both change a lot.

  • The short path is three clicks: Customize, type Booklight, the plus, the keys, Set shortcut. Today’s card sends people through App shortcuts and Add shortcut into a list of every app.
  • A trap today: typing the name before Customize shows “No search results”.
  • The instructions can stand inside the system’s dialog. Opened from a Booklight surface that stays in front, the dialog starts on Booklight’s own page, and its rows are our words. Tried on the Lenovo Googlebook with a stand-in app, in English and German.
  • The new keys close the dialog by themselves. Right after Set shortcut, pressing them makes the dialog fall away and brings the app forward, and the app is told. No Done, no closing. Tried the same way.
  • Action + Quick Insert works (your choice). On the Lenovo the dialog draws the key as a cap that reads “Quick Insert”, takes the combination, and pressing it starts the app and closes the dialog. Nothing else uses it there, and Android 17’s source reserves nothing on it. A keyboard without that key gets Action + J as the suggestion.
  • Action + K is no longer suggested anywhere. Android 17’s source reserves it for a system feature behind a switch.

What you are asked to decide

Settled by you on 4 October

  • The route: the system’s dialog, guided. The key: Action + Quick Insert.
  • The panel stays through the three lessons: first run is one opening, and the keys are pressed once.
  • No row for the device’s model.
  • The opening: Booklight performs before the key step, with a welcome that goes all out in an expanded panel and a flight in the show.
  • Everything else on the first page at its recommended answer: the three functions, the stage, the first click on the icon, the question alone with nothing armed, the blank key that takes the check, “Your key works” waiting for Enter, the light at the reveal and the fold, updates from 3.0, a proof on a device first.

Seen on a device, and not

  • Seen on the Lenovo Googlebook, with a throwaway stand-in for the panel: the dialog opens on the app’s own page; the five rows are drawn as designed, in English and German; the panel’s place lies wholly behind the dialog; closing the dialog gives the focus back; the new keys close the dialog and the app is told; Action + Quick Insert is taken and works; a window that opens an app is put behind it and removed by the system, and comes back in front when it starts itself again at once.
  • Not seen: the real panel behind the dialog; how the step aside and the return look to the eye; what a tall glass full of drawing costs per frame over the window’s blur; anything on the HP Googlebook; the welcome, the show, the screens and the motion, which exist as drawings and as this prototype.

The largest risks

  1. The welcome on a device. No frame time has been measured for a tall glass redrawn every frame over the blur, and matching the prototype’s light is tuning by eye. The first milestone measures it before anything is laid out.
  2. How the panel’s return over a freshly opened app looks. The system fades a see-through window out and in, and that has not been filmed. Practice only is the fallback.
  3. The real panel behind the dialog has never been seen, only the stand-in.
