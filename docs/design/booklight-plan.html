From testing on your HP

BentoBar's crash is fixed in its repo. My earlier use of uiautomator dump suspended every accessibility service and BentoBar 0.5 crashed on unbind. The fix is on kuscher/bentobar main (commit 94a952c, with tests), not released and not yet tried on a device. I no longer use that tool on the HP.

Action + K is still bound to Booklight, as you asked. To try Action + Alt + Space instead, remove the K shortcut under Keyboard shortcuts → App shortcuts and add the new one: the system allows one per app.

What people use a launcher for

I looked at how Spotlight, Alfred and Google's Quick Search Box (the 2009 Mac one and Android's) were built, plus Raycast, PowerToys Run, the ChromeOS launcher and the Android search launchers. No vendor publishes a usage breakdown, so the ranking below rests on one Microsoft survey, three academic studies and individual usage exports. The full reports with sources are in the repo under docs/research/.

What all of them share, and Booklight adopts: one modifier-plus-key toggle, a panel in the upper middle, few rows, the first row always selected and run by Enter, Tab or → for more actions, one ranked list instead of groups, and ranking that learns from picks. From Alfred: the exact text you typed is tied to what you picked for it. From Quick Search Box for Mac: results carry actions, and sources are plug-ins.

The design

You asked for the simplicity of Alfred and Spotlight, a light presence with transparency in the middle of the screen, a Material 3 Expressive look, and motion that makes it feel made with care. After your feedback on the first build, the glass is flat: no bevels or highlights.

  • Glass you can see through. A thin tint of the wallpaper's surface color over the system's blur, and a crisp white outline. Three levels in settings: Clear, Balanced (the default) and Frosted. Apps can blur what is behind a window but not bend it, so there is no true refraction; the outline carries a short gleam when the panel arrives.
  • The field never moves. The panel's top edge sits at 20% of the screen; the list grows down from it on a spring, and the window is exactly the panel.
  • Nothing pops. The glass comes into focus on arrival. Rows rise in as a quick cascade, move to their new places and fade out. One selection pill glides between rows, its leading edge ahead of its trailing edge. Results and actions pass each other sideways. With the system's animations off, everything cuts.
  • Row one is always the answer to Enter. No groups, no tabs. Its name completes in the field. A G stands where the magnifier would be while Google is the search engine.
  • Nothing typed, nothing shown. Only the first two times: a small card under the field for the shortcut, then for search suggestions.

Built to take new abilities

Everything Booklight can find comes from a provider, and everything it can do is an effect described as data. A new ability is one provider file and one line of registration. Because results and effects are plain data, an extension in another app can return the same shapes across the process boundary.

The name

Booklight is Googlebook plus Spotlight, and a book light is a small light you clip on when you need it. The GitHub account name is unclaimed. The largest repo of that name is a 110-star Chrome extension that searches bookmarks Spotlight-style: the same idea in another place, and no obstacle to an Android app. Every dictionary word for light or search is in real use on GitHub, so the alternatives are coinages too.
