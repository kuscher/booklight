One structure for apps

Type the app, Tab to what you want, Enter. If it takes words, type them and press Enter again.

Every app's row has the same places, in the same order. An app only shows the ones it has, so a row carries two to four icons and the arrow, where today it carries six.

  1. OpenEvery app. Enter opens it.
  2. SearchAn app that can be searched. Enter puts the app in the field; type what to look for.
  3. PlayA music app that can play what you name. Enter puts the app in the field; type the song.
  4. WindowEvery app. Enter opens a short list: New window, then the places.
  5. MoreEvery app. Enter opens App info, the app's pages in Settings, Uninstall.

What goes away

Each of these is the same act with a second look today, or a line that the new order makes unnecessary.

  • “Search” as the eleventh line behind the arrow: it is the second place on the row.
  • Spotify's own shortcut row “Search · Spotify” under the app, for apps that have Search on their row.
  • The note symbol and the small Spotify icon inside the action on the play row: the row carries the app's own icon instead, like every row that is about one app.
  • The “Resume” row under play with nothing typed. play and Enter still resumes; pause, next, stop stay.
  • Maximise, Left half, Right half, New window and App info as icons on the row: they are the first lines of Window and of More.

Five things to decide

The designer and the product manager agreed on the order of the row and on everything drawn above. Where they differed, the first answer is the one recommended.

  1. The end of the row. Recommended, and drawn above: one place called Window that opens New window and every placement, and the arrow for the rest. The other answer: New window stays an icon, the two halves and Maximise go behind the arrow with App info and Uninstall, and the ten finer places (thirds, quarters, centre) can only be typed. That is one list instead of two and one key less for a new window, and it takes ten places out of sight.
  2. A sentence that starts with an app's name (netflix stock price, netflix severance). Recommended: the web leads until you have picked the app's row once for that app; after that the app leads, and picking the web twice puts it back. The other answer: the web always leads and nothing is learned, so spotify daft punk and Enter never goes to Spotify again; it is Down, Enter, or the row.
  3. Play only where it really plays. Recommended: Spotify's row has Play once your key is in; without it the row has Search, which is all Spotify does without a key. Other music apps get Play once one of them has been tried on a device.
  4. What play and yt become. Recommended: short ways to the same place. At the space, play becomes the chip of the music app you played in last, and yt becomes YouTube's chip, with the same row as the Tab way. The other answer: they keep their own chips and rows, and there are two looks for one act.
  5. The app's pages in Settings (notifications, language, open by default, battery use). Recommended: all four are lines behind the arrow, since that list is short now, and their typed words stay. The other answer: only Notifications is listed and the other three words go.

Not proposed, though the product manager raised them: turning call, sms, wa and tg off until you switch them on (each already exists only where an app answers it), and moving the editor for your own app commands into Labs.
