Colours that follow what is underneath

Left out, as you chose. Android blurs what is behind the panel itself and gives an app no pixels and no brightness from there; the ways to look are a screen-capture dialog on every opening or an accessibility service. Glass (Clear, Balanced, Frosted, Solid) stays the control for visibility.

How another app adds commands

One line in its manifest and one file. Booklight reads it when the app is installed and matches the words itself, so the app does not start until Enter and never sees what is typed. A command can only open something in its own app.

<booklight version="1">
  <command id="mini" title="@string/mini" keywords="@string/mini_words">
    <open class=".ui.MiniLauncher" />
  </command>
  <scope id="shot" keywords="shot,screenshot" name="@string/capture">
    <command id="area" title="@string/area">
      <open class=".CaptureActivity"><extra name="source" value="area" /></open>
    </command>
  </scope>
</booklight>

StudioSnap, Summa and the others would each need a small release of their own to carry this file. That is not part of Booklight 2.0.

The plan

Fifteen tasks, each ending with the tests green, the build green, the feature driven once on the Lenovo, and a commit. Two reviewers read the whole change at the end, as for 1.0 and 1.1.

  1. Core. Places as arithmetic, the text of the notes files, prompts, three-word verbs; all with tests.
  2. Places. Seventeen actions on an app's row, their words in English and German, the other display.
  3. The row's second line. Nine, the arrow, the line below, one highlight that travels between them.
  4. Other apps' commands. What apps declare, the file for Booklight, the checks before anything is started, the switches.
  5. s, k and Tab on a keyword. More settings pages, checked on the device; the hand-over to the Settings app's search.
  6. The system's keys. The table of 46 (written already), rows with key caps.
  7. Notes. Find, tasks, another file, today's file.
  8. Prompts. Five to start with, a scope each, rows for handed-over text, the editor.
  9. Pin. The permission, the small window, the countdown.
  10. The guide. One table behind ?, the Commands page and the tips.
  11. Tips. The card, the switch.
  12. The window. The column, the pages, "No key yet".
  13. Finish. German, TalkBack, version 2.0, the documents, the store's notes.
  14. On the device. Every new row typed key by key; the second line, the column and the tip card stepped through frame by frame; the pinned layer; places by their real bounds; German and dark.
  15. Reviews, then the release. A design review and a code review, their findings fixed, then the tag.

Not checked: a second display (no monitor at the desk), and the HP, which is offline. The release notes will say so.
