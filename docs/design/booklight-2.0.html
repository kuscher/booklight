What the reviews changed

All three said the same thing first: the ideas fit Booklight, and the first draft would not have been built to 1.1's standard. What changed because of them:

They foundNow
An opened row had no room: eight results and eight actions are sixteen rows on a screen that holds twelve.While a row is open the list is that row and its actions. The others fade, as when a scope is entered.
The action rows were indented onto a line that exists nowhere else; nine icons left the title 130 dp.Action rows sit on the panel's two existing lines. Icons at a 32 dp pitch; the title has a fixed box and fades.
A held Tab would have opened and shut the list every 850 ms.The arrow is a stop called More. Only Enter, a second →, or a click opens it.
new tab would have made a file called "tab": new is a keyword.Inside a scope, a command whose name starts with the keyword and the text comes first.
Tab on a keyword "whichever row is selected" took Tab away from a row you had moved to.Tab enters the keyword only while the selection is where typing left it.
Tips made Enter live on an empty panel; Enter twice would have started the example.Nothing is armed until Tab. After Booklight fills the field, the next Enter must be a new press.
The tip card would have changed the opening itself, every time.The panel opens as always. The tip arrives 0.4 s later, and only if nothing was typed.
Ticking a task showed no tick, and a second Enter ticked the next one.The check draws itself and the row stays, with Undo.
The window had two identical highlights, divider lines and yellow "New" marks.The column's highlight is quiet glass, the page keeps the one coloured pill, no lines, no "New".
Left third and left half were 2 px apart at the device's size.A third is drawn as three columns: a different shape from a half.

Answers on this device

Measured on the Lenovo today, from Booklight's own process: the Googlebook's on-device model (Gemini Nano) answers a plain app. So the prompts can be answered in their own row instead of in the Gemini app.

Half a second after you stop typing, the model is asked. While it works the panel's edge light runs; the words fade in as they arrive. Enter copies. Typing again cancels. Nothing leaves the device.

The five

  1. Fix and rewrite: fix …, shorter …, friendly …. Replace puts it back into the field it came from.
  2. Translate: de …, en ….
  3. Summarise what you copied or what another app handed over: sum.
  4. A short answer: explain idempotent, with “Open in Gemini” beside it.
  5. Say it plainly: “remind me tomorrow at nine to call the bank”. The model proposes, Booklight's parsers decide. Larger; for the release after.

1 to 4 are one mechanism, about a week; your own prompts get it for free.

What it costs. One more install-time permission (the system's AI service; no prompt). Google's library reports usage to Google from a background job; I took that part out on the Lenovo and the model still answered, nothing was scheduled, and “no analytics, nothing in the background” stayed true. Google has not called that supported. Google's terms for these APIs exclude apps for people under 18, so Booklight's Play audience would move from 13 to 18 and over.

The Booklight window

Seven sections and one page beside them, on one ground with no lines. The column's highlight is quiet glass and says where you are; the page's is the coloured pill and says where the keys are. Click the sections: the page follows the pill and comes from the side the pill came from.

How it moves

The motion review wrote a table of 35 rows in the design system's format, each with how it is interrupted, what it does with animations off, and what would pop if built carelessly. Everything rides 1.1's springs; two new named specs are added.

MomentWhat movesSpec
The row unfoldsOne edge uncovers rows laid out once; the window's height follows the same value; the chevron turns; the pill pours in.place, pop, lead/trail
Booklight typesLetters land one by one; the keyword becomes its chip when its space lands; one search at the end.new: type, 480 ms in all
The check that draws itselfOne stroke wherever Booklight says done: a task, “Copied”, “Your key works”.140 ms
A typed placeThe arrow's slot becomes the action; the pane goes straight there; no name in between unrolls.arm
Tab on a keywordThe keyword's letters slide into the chip; text and chip change in one frame; the pill does not move.place, pop
The tipThe opening is unchanged; the card's parts rise in after it. Gone in the frame of the first letter.place, 22 ms apart
The page changesThe old page fades where it stands; the new follows the column's pill by 60 ms.fade 70; place
A countdown digitOnly the digits that changed, 8 dp; one wake a second.new: tick, 160 ms
ClosingAs 1.1.1, drawn in to the field's centre line whatever the panel's height.155 ms

Every row gets stepped through frame by frame on the device, with a debug-only slow factor for all springs.

The plan

Sixteen tasks with on-device answers, fifteen without. Each ends with the tests green, the build green, the feature driven once on the Lenovo, and a commit. The same three reviewers read the built app at the end.

  1. Core. Places, the text of the notes files, prompts, three-word verbs; with tests.
  2. Motion's ground. The new specs in one file, the slow factor, the footer on the window's bottom edge, the opening's gate.
  3. Places and the row. Seventeen actions, ten slots at a 32 dp pitch, More, the list that unfolds.
  4. Other apps' commands. What apps declare, the file for Booklight, the checks before anything starts, the switches.
  5. s, k and Tab on a keyword. More settings pages, checked on the device.
  6. The system's keys. The table of 46 (written), rows with caps in one column.
  7. Notes. Find, tasks with the tick and Undo, another file, today's file.
  8. Prompts, and answers on this device if you say yes.
  9. Pin. The permission, the window, the countdown.
  10. The guide and Booklight typing. One table behind ?, the Commands page and the tips.
  11. Tips.
  12. The window. The column, the pages, “No key yet”.
  13. Finish. German, TalkBack, version 2.0, the documents.
  14. On the device. The motion review's fifteen checks, frame by frame.
  15. Reviews of the built app: use, look, motion, code. Findings fixed.
  16. The release. Tag v2.0.

Decide

  1. Answers on this device.In 2.0, with the usage reporting taken out recommended; or after 2.0; or not at all.
  2. The 18-and-over term.Needed for the on-device answers on Play. Move Booklight's Play audience to 18 and over, or keep the answers out of the Play build.
  3. Tips.Tab then Enter tries one, and they end after one pass recommended by the review; or Enter tries at once and they never end.
  4. The plan and the design.Approve, or say what to change.
