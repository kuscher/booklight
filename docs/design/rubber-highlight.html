Rubber highlight

The highlight behind the selected row, in three versions. Today is what Booklight does now. A and B make it more like rubber: the front edge goes first, the old edge holds on for a moment and then follows. A is the one proposed; B is the same idea one step stronger, to show how much is enough.

Two things to decide: A, B, or leave it as it is today; and whether the square in the emoji grid takes the same motion in the same release. Nothing is built into the app until you say.

What to try

  • One step, then the same step in the other two versions. Quarter speed shows what the old edge does.
  • Hold the key. In A and B the highlight keeps its shape and stays close to the selected row. Today it falls behind and gets long. That is what the code does in this simulation; it has not been filmed on a device.
  • Down and straight back up. The highlight turns round from where it is; it never jumps.
  • Onto the tall row and off it. The highlight grows to the row's height on the way.
  • Click a row far away. In A and B the highlight is never more than one row (56 dp) longer than its own row, however far it goes, and it arrives in one move.

The rows are examples. Today is drawn from the numbers in Booklight's code. Why it falls behind on a held key in this simulation: when a new key arrives while the highlight is still moving, the code starts both of its edges again from a standstill. That was found by reading the code and testing the library it uses. Nobody has filmed it on a Googlebook yet.

What this changes, and what it leaves alone

Only the list's highlight changes. The same kind of highlight exists in three more places: the square in the emoji grid, the pane on a row's actions, and the rail of the Booklight window.

  • The grid's square is built the same way, from two edges. It could take A in the same release.
  • The pane on a row's actions is built differently: one movement carries the pane and the names that unroll beside it. It needs its own look before it changes.
  • The window's rail can follow later.

Which of them follow, and when, is yours to decide. At full speed A and B are close; the quarter-speed switch shows the difference. If you cannot tell them apart at full speed, A is enough.
