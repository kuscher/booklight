Two things you should know from testing

I broke BentoBar for about three minutes, and fixed it. To read the system's Keyboard shortcuts window I used uiautomator dump, which suspends every accessibility service. BentoBar 0.5 throws when its service is unbound, crashed twice, and Android showed "BentoBar keeps stopping". I dismissed the dialog and re-added BentoBar's entry to the enabled services (the other two were kept); its strip is back and all three services are bound. I no longer use that tool on the HP. The crash itself is a BentoBar bug (question 14).

Action + K is bound to Booklight on your HP. I added it in Keyboard shortcuts so the hotkey could be tested for real. Remove it there under App shortcuts, or tell me to (question 13).

What people use a launcher for

I looked at how Spotlight, Alfred and Google's Quick Search Box (the 2009 Mac one and Android's) were built, plus Raycast, PowerToys Run, the ChromeOS launcher and the Android search launchers. No vendor publishes a usage breakdown, so the ranking below rests on one Microsoft survey, three academic studies and individual usage exports. The full reports with sources are in the repo under docs/research/.

What all of them share, and Booklight adopts: one modifier-plus-key toggle, a panel in the upper middle, few rows, the first row always selected and run by Enter, Tab or → for more actions, one ranked list instead of groups, and ranking that learns from picks. From Alfred: the exact text you typed is tied to what you picked for it. From Quick Search Box for Mac: results carry actions, and sources are plug-ins.

Versions

1.0 · the launcher no permissions

  • Summon with Action + a key (guided setup), the taskbar or the Apps list. The same key closes it.
  • Every app, work profile included. Starts, later words, initials, camelCase, loose letters.
  • Learns per typed text, with a four-week memory.
  • Nothing typed: your most-used apps as a strip.
  • The top hit completes in the field as grey text.
  • Tab lists a row's actions: Open, App info, Uninstall, Store page, Copy.
  • Sums in place. About 35 settings pages by name and keyword.
  • Typed addresses, a web search row, keyword searches (g yt w maps gh play and your own).
  • The device's colours and font, light and dark, glass with a solid fallback.

2.x · more to find and do none or light

  • Extensions: other apps offer results and actions. First: Summa (units, money, dates, time zones), BentoBar (timer, keep awake), StudioSnap (capture), PDF Toolbox, HearOn Link.
  • App shortcuts ("New incognito tab", "New sheet").
  • Quick actions through public intents: timer, calendar event, email, navigate.
  • Files in folders you grant. Contacts, when first used.
  • Emoji, snippets, your own quick links.
  • A desktop search pill widget and a Quick Settings tile.

3.x · advanced each opt-in

  • Assistant role: Action + Space and the Assistant key open Booklight. Replaces Gemini as the default assistant, so it is a clear choice, never a default.
  • Accessibility tier: a list of open windows, clipboard history, system commands, pasting snippets, any key combination.
  • Usage access: rank by what you use system-wide.
  • On-device AI (Gemini Nano): typed requests turned into actions, still without an internet permission.

The design

You asked for the simplicity of Alfred and Spotlight, a light presence with transparency in the middle of the screen, and a Material 3 Expressive look. The design is one panel and one list:

  • The field never moves. The panel's top edge sits at 20% of the screen; the list grows down from it. The window is exactly the panel, so the blur and the click-outside area are the panel too.
  • Glass, not a sheet. The surface is 78% of the wallpaper-derived surface colour over the system's blur, with a hairline border. The desktop dims by 16% and stays readable. When the system turns blur off, the same layout sits on a solid surface.
  • Expressive where it carries meaning. Rows are pills. The selected one is a tonal pill with tighter corners that glides between rows on a spring. Answers use the rounded cut of Google Sans Flex. Colour comes from the wallpaper. Nothing else animates: the panel appears and goes in the same frame as the key.
  • Row one is always the answer to Enter. No groups, no tabs. A row's kind is a quiet word at its right edge; the selected row shows what Enter will do instead.

Built to take new abilities

Everything Booklight can find comes from a provider, and everything it can do is an effect described as data. A new ability is one provider file and one line of registration. Because results and effects are plain data, an extension in another app can return the same shapes across the process boundary.

The name

Booklight is Googlebook plus Spotlight, and a book light is a small light you clip on when you need it. The GitHub account name is unclaimed. The largest repo of that name is a 110-star Chrome extension that searches bookmarks Spotlight-style: the same idea in another place, and no obstacle to an Android app. Every dictionary word for light or search is in real use on GitHub, so the alternatives are coinages too.
