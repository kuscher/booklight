The model in seven rules

  1. One field over one list. The field holds at most one chip, then text. Row one is selected.
  2. A row shows what it can do. The selected row carries its actions as chips at its right end. One is armed and shows the Enter mark; Enter runs it.
  3. Tab moves to the next thing the row offers. Its next action, or, if the row takes text, typing into it. → at the end of the text does the same.
  4. A typed verb arms an action. "chrome uninstall" and "uninstall chrome" are the Chrome row with Uninstall armed. Never a second row.
  5. A scope is a row that takes text. Entering it turns its keyword into a chip where the G is. Backspace on an empty field turns it back.
  6. A control is a row with a state. ← → change it live and the panel stays; Enter commits and closes.
  7. Reversible things are live; permanent or outward things wait. Delete needs Enter twice. Uninstall is never armed by default and Android asks before it happens.

The full model with the key map and the sources it draws on (Chrome's omnibox, Raycast, Alfred, Spotlight, PowerToys) is in the repo: docs/design/ux-model.md.

Three limits the research found

Do Not Disturb is narrower than it sounds: an app built for Android 15 or later can only switch its own mode on and off. It cannot switch off Do Not Disturb that you or another app turned on.

Notes in a plain file don't survive a reinstall: Android stops treating the old file as Booklight's. A folder you grant once avoids that.

Keep awake without a background service (which Play reviews with a video) means raising the screen timeout while it is on, using the same Settings switch as brightness.

Uninstall, and what Play asks for

Uninstall needs REQUEST_DELETE_PACKAGES: an install-time permission with no prompt. Android still shows its own "Do you want to uninstall this app?" dialog every time. Play asks for nothing extra: no form, no declaration; other launchers on Play declare it. Without the permission the uninstall silently does nothing, so it can't be done "for free".

Your three additions

Four openings to compare. Unfold is your idea: a line grows up and down, then the glass opens out of it to both sides and settles with a small bounce. The contents are laid out once and only uncovered, so nothing reflows. Bloom grows the glass out of a small pill in every direction at once. Bigger bounce is 1.0's arrival with about twice the bounce. The edge light can run as in 1.0, about half as fast, or once around the outline. Use Replay and Quarter speed above the panel. Typing is live from the first frame in all of them, and a setting turns the animation off.

A Booklight window for the app icon. Today the icon appears to do nothing. I found why: started from the Apps list, the panel opens and closes itself within a moment, because the Apps list closing takes focus away. That is a bug to fix either way. The proposal: the icon opens a normal window that says what Booklight is, shows how to give it a key, and holds the settings, drawn with the panel's own components and motion. The keyboard shortcut still opens the panel (Android tells the app who started it, so the two can be told apart).

Ask Gemini. Checked on the HP: handing text to the Gemini app opens it with your text in the prompt, not sent, so one more Enter there sends it. No permission needed. In Booklight a long or question-like text gets "Ask Gemini" as its first row; for anything else it is one row under "Search Google". ask then Tab makes it a scope. Gemini's own actions (new chat, Live, notebooks) can't be listed by another app today; the ones with public links can be added as rows.
