What each part means

  • Line one is the flight: number, airline and the two cities.
  • The large line is the one thing you came for: when it leaves, or how long until it lands.
  • The badge says whether that is the plan: On time, Delayed, early, or Planned when only the plan is known. A cancelled flight has no badge: the word is the large line.
  • The line is the flight from take-off to landing. The solid part is behind the plane, the dots are still to come. No plane means nobody knows where it is.
  • Under the line’s ends: the airport and its time, in that airport’s own clock, and in small words what matters at that end now: gate and terminal before it leaves; after, the arrival terminal, and at the start the aircraft when the service names it.

What is real and what is worked out

  • From the flight service: the airports, the planned, expected and actual times, terminal and gate, the aircraft, and whether it is planned, in the air, landed or cancelled.
  • Worked out in Booklight: the plane’s place (the share of the flying time that has passed; time, not distance on a map), “Leaves in” and “Lands in” (the same times against your clock), and the badge (the time shown against the plan).
  • Never shown as known: a gate, a belt or an aircraft the service left out. Their words are simply not there, and nothing leaves a gap.
  • The flight on this page is an example, and its times are made up.

What you are asked to decide

  1. Colour in the badge. Green for on time and early, amber for late, nowhere else: the line and the plane stay in the row’s own ink. We recommend it. It changes two of your rules: “a delay is said, not painted” and “the selection is the only coloured surface”. The last switch shows the row without it.
  2. When “late” starts. We recommend 15 minutes after the plan (today 5): with a colour on it, “Delayed 6 min” is alarm for nothing, and the exact time stands at the line’s end either way.
  3. Cancelled without red. The word alone, the times struck, no plane. Red stays the colour of what removes something. If you want it louder, it becomes a red badge.
  4. The height. 136, the height of an answer that has grown; the most we would go to is 148.
  5. The aircraft in small words under the line’s start once it has left, and no altitude or speed. Say so if you want them.
