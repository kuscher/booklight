## 3. Other apps' commands

Two static layers; no other app's process starts before Enter, and no app sees what is typed.

**What apps already declare.** Each launcher activity's `android.app.shortcuts` XML (the items on its icon's
right-click menu), read with the app list. A shortcut becomes a row if its last intent resolves to an activity
**in the declaring package** that is **exported** and asks for **no permission**. On the Lenovo: 28 declared, 19
usable. Skipped: the Settings app's (Booklight has those pages), and any whose title is one of Booklight's own
actions for that app (the Terminal's "New Window" is the row's second icon already).

**One file for Booklight.** `<meta-data android:name="io.github.kuscher.booklight.commands"
android:resource="@xml/…"/>` on the `<application>`; `command`, `scope` with fixed `command`s, `scope` with an
`open` that takes `{argument}`. Format and rules in `docs/EXTENSIONS.md`. The debug build declares one for itself.
His own apps each need a small release to carry such a file: not part of 2.0.

**Rows.** The app's icon; title = the command; **the kind label at the right is the app's name** (one line: the app
is named once in words, not three times). Rules, from the reviews:
- Found by the command's title, by "app command", and by its file keywords (which count less than a title, eight
  per command at most).
- By the app's name alone: only for the app that is row one, with three letters or more typed, three commands at
  most, and never in place of a row that matched on its own name. (`k` does not list every command of every app on K.)
- A typed verb wins over a command: `chrome new` is Chrome with New window armed, as in 1.1.
- **Inside a scope, a command whose title starts with the keyword and the text comes first** (two at most, from two
  letters after the keyword): `new tab` shows "New tab · Chrome" above New's own preview. Without this rule the
  headline example would make a file called "tab".
- Equal titles from two apps: the app used most first; icon and name always shown.
- Another app's scope keyword is entered with Tab or Enter on its row; the keyword and a Space enters it only once
  the user has entered it that way before. It never takes a keyword Booklight or the user has.

**Running one.** `Effect.Open(owner, intent)`: an `intent:` URI, parsed and started by the `Executor` only if it
resolves to an exported activity of `owner` that needs no permission; flags are its own, no grant flags, no clip
data, no selector.

**Switches** (Results page): "Commands from your apps", then each app that offers any. With the first off the app
rows dim to 0.40 and the pill skips them; they do not fold away.

## 4. Pin

`Effect.Pin(kind, text, value)` opens `pin/PinActivity`: a small, opaque, ordinary window that asks for the pinned
layer (`AppTask.requestWindowingLayer(WINDOWING_LAYER_PINNED)`). One at a time: a new pin replaces the old one
(the same window when the kind is the same: the old content fades, the new rises). If the layer is refused the
window stays, as a normal one.

| On | The window shows | Size (content) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `pin buy milk`, and Pin on a note or a found line | the text, six lines at most; Copy | 320 × (68 + 24 per line) |
| a sum's answer | the sum, small; the answer; Copy | 280 × 128 |
| a colour | the swatch; its four forms as a vertical option strip (Enter copies the chosen one) | 280 × 164 |
| a QR code | the code on a white plate; Copy image | 216 × 244 |
| a timer: "Start and pin" | "tea · set for 14:12", then the countdown; the Clock app's timer starts and rings | 280 × 88 |

- **The system's caption is the only bar**; the task's label is the kind. No title bar of our own. One left edge at
  20 dp. The caption comes first, the figure under it, in every kind.
- **Copy** is an action strip of one at the bottom right; the highlight shows only while the window has the keys or
  the pointer. "Copied" with the drawn check, in place.
- **The keys stay with the app the user was in.** The pin does not keep the keyboard after it has asked for its
  layer; a click gives it the keys, Esc then closes it. (To check on a device: the layer is granted only to a focused
  window.)
- `pin` with nothing typed lists what is pinned, with "Unpin". A new pin that would replace a text says so on its
  row ("Replaces “Gate B22…”"): a pinned text exists nowhere else.
- The countdown changes one digit at a time (`tick`), stops at 0:00, swells once; one wake a second, none while the
  window is hidden. "set for", not "rings at": a timer cancelled in the Clock still counts here.

**As built (the device decided, 1 October).** The pin is a picture-in-picture window, not a window in the pinned
layer: a pinned-layer window takes the keyboard when it opens and again every time the panel closes, and an app
cannot hand it back (`docs/research/device-findings.md`). Picture-in-picture is the one kind of window the system
keeps on top without ever giving it the keys, which is what this section asks for. What follows from that:

- No permission (`USE_PINNED_WINDOWING_LAYER` is not asked for). No caption: the system draws the card, moves it
  and closes it (its own cross, on a tap or under the pointer).
- Sizes: 280 × 118 dp for an answer, a countdown and up to three lines of text; 24 dp more per line up to six;
  280 × 140 for a colour (its swatch, the form it was typed in, and the other three under it); a QR code square.
  The user can drag it larger; the content grows with it as one piece.
- The window is for looking at. **Copy and Unpin are in the panel**, on the row `pin` shows for what is pinned
  (the system shows an app's own buttons only on a window some 220 dp high). No option strip for a colour's forms,
  no Enter or Esc in the pin: it never has the keys.
- A countdown has a line under the time that shortens with it.
- Opened out by the user (the system's expand control) it is an ordinary small window: a click or Enter copies,
  Esc closes.
- The arrival is the system's (the window goes on top its own way, about half a second); the content then fades in
  once. One pin at a time, and one picture-in-picture window at a time on the device: a video in
  picture-in-picture and a pin replace each other.

## 5. The system's keys

`res/values/keys_table.xml` (English and German, 46 entries, from the Welcome repo's device notes). `snap`, `desk`,
`screenshot` find rows that show the combination as key caps: the panel's own cap (24 dp, no rim), "+" between
them. The caps keep one place in every row, selected or not: the strip's room is kept free at their right, and the
kind label ("Key") and the strip trade places inside it. Enter on a row is "All shortcuts ⏎" (the system's Keyboard
shortcuts window: Booklight cannot press the keys, and the name says so); the second action copies the combination.
A switch on the Results page turns them off. `k` is their scope (§11).

## 6. Places and a row's other actions

`Place` grows by left, middle and right third, left and right two thirds, the four quarters, centre and full
(`core/Places.kt`, arithmetic on the work area, tested). Place names are short, 12 characters at most ("Left ⅔",
"Links ⅔", "Middle ⅓", "Top left"). "… on display 2" opens on that display when there is one; with one display
the words are ordinary text. A window that is already open keeps its place, as in 1.1: the tips, `?` and the
release notes say "when the app is not open yet". Recipes can hold the new places. **Store page is gone** (action,
verb and effect).

**The row.** Ten slots at most, flush right as in 1.1, at a 32 dp pitch (18 dp icons at ink 1.0), the armed one
unrolled to its name and the Enter mark:

`Open · New window · App info · Left half · Right half · Full · Left third · Middle third · Right third · More`

1.1's first five keep their places. The title's box is fixed per row (what the widest state of its strip leaves:
170 dp in English, 148 in German) and ends in a 24 dp fade, so the title is laid out once and never swaps glyphs
while a name unrolls. If nine icons read as a blur on the device, the fallback is seven and the arrow at 1.1's pitch.

**More** is a stop like the others: a chevron; armed, it reads "More ⏎". Tab and Shift + Tab walk the stops and
wrap, → walks without wrapping and stops on More, exactly as in 1.1. **Moving the arming never opens anything**, so
a held key cannot pump the panel.

**Opening.** Enter on More, → again on More, or a click on the chevron (first press only). The row opens the way the
panel does, out of one edge:
- While a row is open **the list is that row and its other actions, nothing else**: the other results fade where
  they are (as when a scope is entered), the row rides to first place if it was not there.
- The action rows are laid out once, under the row, and **uncovered by one edge** that travels down on `place`; the
  window's height follows the same value. The chevron turns over on `pop`. No stagger: the edge is the cascade.
- Each action row is 40 dp: a bare 18 dp glyph centred on the icon column (x = 38), its name from the title's edge
  (x = 72) in the strip's own type (14/500), ink 0.80. No indent, no disc, no kind label: they are the strip's slots,
  one per line, on the panel's two existing lines.
- The list's one pill, at full width, pours into the first action row (lower edge on `lead`, upper on `trail`). The
  selected action shows an Enter mark in the arrow's column (x = 684). No pane in the list: the pill is its highlight.
- The opened row itself keeps only its icon, its name and the chevron, turned up, while the pill is below it.
- **Uninstall** is last, 8 dp apart, in error ink; under the pill, the pill takes the error container's colour.
  Android's own dialog stays the confirmation. Ctrl + digit and arrows never run it.
- In the list: ↓ ↑ move the pill (↑ from the first row goes back to the row, "Less ⏎" armed); Tab and Shift + Tab
  are ↓ and ↑, wrapping inside the list; ← closes; Enter runs; Shift + Enter runs and stays; Esc closes the panel.
  Typing closes the list in the same frame and edits the text. Hover selects, never closes.
- Closing is opening backwards: the edge travels up, the pill returns to the row as one rigid piece, More armed.
- Height: 56 + 7 × 40 + 8 + 40 = 384 dp, under what eight results take (448).

Under the arrow: `Left ⅔ · Right ⅔ · Top left · Top right · Bottom left · Bottom right · Centre · Uninstall`. The
split is by action, not by count: an app without New window has eight icons and the same list.

**A typed place** ("chrome top left", "files right third", "code full") has its action ready. Among the nine: armed
where it stands, the pane going straight there (no name in between unrolls). Beyond the nine: **the arrow's slot
becomes that action** (the chevron cross-fades to its glyph, the name unrolls, the nine slide left by exactly that);
never an eleventh slot. Delete the words and the chevron is back. `chrome uninstall` shows Uninstall there, in error
ink, as in 1.1 only when asked for by name. While a place is being typed the row never blinks out: a last word of
one letter that starts one of the row's verbs leaves the name reading standing ("chrome t").

## 7. The list of everything

`?` as the first character is the scope at once, without a Space. Every keyword and kind of row, with an example;
typing narrows. Enter on a keyword's row enters it ("Type… ⏎", chip to chip); on anything else the action is
"Try it ⏎": **Booklight types the example**, a letter at a time (`type`: 480 ms for the whole, 16 to 40 ms a
letter), so the keyword becomes its chip in front of the user; one search at the end. Nothing typed: what has not
been used yet. The last row, "All commands", opens the Commands page of the window (§10). No web row in this
scope. "help" finds the scope's row but is not a keyword ("help with taxes" is a search).

**One guard for everything that fills the field for you** (Try it, a `?` row, a Commands row, Enter on a scope's
row): the next Enter runs only as a new press at least 350 ms later, and text put there by Booklight is not kept as
"the last text" unless the user edits it.

## 8. Notes

- `notes milk` (also `find`): lines of Notes.md, newest first; nothing typed, the latest. Copy · Pin · Open file.
  Rows are keyed by their line, so narrowing moves them instead of re-drawing them. The first rows come from a
  file: the list's height holds until they are known and then moves once.
- `todo call bank`: row one is "Add “call bank”"; under it the open tasks that contain the text. `todo` alone: the
  open tasks, in file order. A task row has a 20 dp box in the icon column and its date at the right.
  **Enter ticks it and the row stays where it is**: the check draws itself, a line strikes the title from its
  start, the ink goes to 0.60, the action becomes "Undo", the panel stays open. Enter again unticks. Ticked rows
  are gone the next time the scope is entered. A task is its line in the file, not its text.
- `note ideas better onboarding`: when the whole first word is the name of another `.md` file in the folder (not
  Todo.md, not a daily file), a second row adds the rest to that file. The default stays Notes.md.
- The note row gains "Today's note": the line goes to `2026-10-01.md`; the footer names the file.
- No folder yet: one row, "Choose the notes folder…", as `note` has. The folder is listed when a notes scope is
  entered, never on a keystroke and never otherwise.

## 9. Prompts

A prompt is a name, a keyword and a text. Five to start with: `fix` (spelling and grammar), `shorter`, `de` or `en`
(translate, by the device's language), `sum` (summarise), `explain`. They are edited in the window like snippets.
`{text}` says where the text goes; without it, it goes after. A seeded prompt whose keyword the user already uses
is seeded without one.

The row is 1.1's 92 dp preview: the prompt's name, then the text at ink 1.0 on two lines. With nothing typed it
takes the clipboard and **shows its first line** under "From the clipboard"; an empty or sensitive clipboard gives
a row with no action. The web search for the whole text stays second ("fix bike tyre" is a search). The same prompts
are rows for text another app hands over (after Search and Ask Gemini) and can be found by name in `clip`.

Where the answer comes from is §13. Without §13: Enter opens the Gemini app with the prompt and the text.

## 10. The Booklight window

A column of sections and one page beside it, on one ground, with no lines between them:

| Section | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| Start | the title and lead, the live stage, **Your key**, Tips |
| Commands | the list of everything (§7): groups, a name, one line, the example at the right as plain text |
| Yours | links, snippets, recipes, prompts |
| Look | theme, colours, glass, dim, opening |
| Results | search engine, suggestions, settings pages, sums, Ask Gemini, the system's keys, other apps |
| Access | notes folder, brightness, assistant key |
| About | privacy policy, version, what Booklight keeps; "Forget everything" last |

- **Measures.** Column item 48 dp high, 200 wide, icon 20, name 15/600. Page 720 wide; column and page centred as a
  group from 1008 dp of window; under that the column's width follows the window's (never animated, never a
  threshold: labels are covered behind a fade) down to 48 dp of icons. Page title 36/700 on the first item's centre
  line. Rows are 1.1's `PageRow`; every row of Results has a mark, so all text starts on one edge. No "New" marks,
  no chips with a fill, no fixed hues.
- **Two highlights that cannot be mistaken.** The column's is quiet glass (the pane 1.1 uses for a choice on a row):
  it says where you are and travels between sections on `lead` and `trail`. The page's is the one coloured pill: it
  says where the keys are. When the keys are in the column, its pane takes a 2 dp ring and the page's pill fades in
  place; nothing travels between the two.
- **Keys.** Tab and Shift + Tab move between column and page. ← on a row without a control also goes to the column;
  on a control ← and → change it and never leave the page. In the column ↑ ↓ change the section at once; → or Enter
  goes into the page.
- **The page changes** after the pill: the old page fades where it stands (70), the new one follows the column's
  pill by 60 ms and rises from the side the pill came from, its blocks 22 ms apart. A held arrow shows no pages in
  between.
- **Commands page.** Enter on a row opens the panel and Booklight types the example (§7).
- **Your key.** "No key yet", with a small dot on the section, until the panel has once been opened the way a key
  (or the assistant key) opens it. Seeded true for anyone who already used 1.1. After "Open Keyboard shortcuts"
  the line reads "Now press the key once". When the window next has the keys after that, the line changes in place
  to "Your key works", with the drawn check; nothing under it moves. The panel's own "Give Booklight a key" card
  follows the same flag.

## 11. `s` and `k`

- **`s`: Settings.** Every settings page Booklight knows (1.1's 35 plus the ones the system offers that were
  missing, each checked to exist on the device). The last local row hands over to the Settings app's own search.
- **`k`: Keys.** The 46 system shortcuts of §5. Its last row is "All shortcuts", the system's window.
- Nothing typed in either: what was opened from Booklight lately, then a fixed set.
- When nothing matches the text ("s bahn", "k pop"), row one is the web search for the whole text and the hand-over
  row is second: the Space may capture an ordinary word, and Enter still does what was meant.
- German keeps `s` and `k` (plus `settings`, `einstellungen`, `keys`, `tasten`, `kürzel`). No other letters.

**Tab on a keyword.** When the field holds exactly a scope's keyword and the selection is where typing left it,
Tab makes the keyword the chip (first press only; the keyword's letters travel into the chip, text and chip in one
frame). Once ↓ ↑ ← → or the pointer has moved the selection, Tab is the selected row's again, as in 1.1. The
footer's hint then reads "tab Search settings". The keyword and a Space enters too, as in 1.1.

**Ranking.** The row of an exact one-letter keyword ranks under every app, setting and control that matches and
keeps the last local place, with its `tab` cap. So `s`, Enter opens the usual app. This changes `g` and `w` too
(`w`, Enter used to enter Wikipedia); the release notes say so.

**Who owns a keyword.** The user's things cannot share one with each other. `s` and `k` yield to a user's link
called `s` or `k` (the link keeps the letter; the editor says "Replaces Booklight's s. `settings` still works.").
Booklight's other keywords stay reserved. Another app never takes one that Booklight or the user has.

## 12. Tips

A switch on the Start page, on from the start. Once the two first-run cards are done:

- **The opening does not change.** The panel unfolds at the field's height, always. If nothing has been typed about
  400 ms after it opened, the tip arrives: the height grows on `place` and the card's parts rise and fade in, 22 ms
  apart. Someone who types at once never sees a card and the tip is not counted as shown.
- **The card** is the keyboard-shortcut reminder's: the feature's own mark on the row's disc, a name ("Timer"), one
  line that starts with what to type, in strong ink, and says what happens in a few words (90 characters at most,
  one feature per tip). **The example also stands in the field as its placeholder**, where typing happens.
- **Answers:** "Try it" and "Turn off tips". Nothing is armed at rest: Enter on an empty panel does nothing, as in
  1.1. Tab arms "Try it" (the card shows the `tab` cap), Tab again "Turn off tips"; Enter does the armed one.
  "Try it": Booklight types the example (§7) and the card goes after the last letter. "Turn off tips": the line
  rolls to "Tips are off. Turn them on in Booklight's window.", then the card folds.
- **Which tip.** The same table as §7, `?` first. A tip stays the tip until it has been on screen three seconds in
  all, or was tried; then the next. Features already used are skipped. After one pass they end; "Show the tips
  again" is a row in the window, and a new version brings new ones.
- Closing with a card showing is the 1.1.1 closing, drawn in to the field's centre line.

## 13. Answers on this device

**Measured on the Lenovo** (user build, from Booklight's own process, no prompt): AICore serves ordinary apps.
Model `nano-v3`, 8,192 tokens. Loading about 2 s once; then the first word after about 0.3 s and 70 to 100
characters a second. English to German, German to English, a grammar fix, a friendlier rewrite, a two-sentence
explanation and pulling a date and a place out of a sentence all came back right, in 0.7 to 3.7 s. It got one name
wrong in a summary ("Google Books" for Googlebooks). Only the app in front is served: the panel is. The system's
own translator is not offered to apps (checked); the system's text classifier is (language, dates, links, flights).
The HP is unchecked.

**The idea.** The prompts of §9 are answered in their own row instead of in the Gemini app:
- In a prompt's scope, after a pause of about half a second in typing (three characters or more), the answer
  streams into the preview row at ink 1.0: words fade in as they arrive, the row grows once if it needs a third and
  fourth line, never more. Typing again cancels it. Enter copies; the next actions are Pin and "Open in Gemini";
  for text another app handed over from an editable field, **Replace** puts the answer back there.
- The row says "On this device". Nothing typed leaves the device, and nothing is asked per keystroke.
- No model on the device (or not yet downloaded): the row is §9's hand-over to Gemini. One row, "Get the on-device
  model", starts the download in the system's own service and says how large it is.

**The five**, in the order they would be built:
1. **Fix and rewrite** (`fix …`, `shorter …`, `friendly …`): the corrected text, large; Enter copies; Replace.
2. **Translate** (`de good morning, are we still on for lunch?`, `en …`): the translation; Enter copies.
3. **Summarise** what you copied or what another app handed over (`sum`): one or two lines.
4. **A short answer** (`explain idempotent`): two lines, with "Open in Gemini" beside it for anything about the world.
5. **Say it plainly** ("remind me tomorrow at nine to call the bank", "open chrome on the left and set a 10 minute
   timer"): the model proposes, Booklight's own parsers decide, and the result is an ordinary row. Only when
   Booklight's parsers found nothing. Larger; proposed for the release after, starting with dates and reminders.

1 to 4 are one mechanism (the prompts, answered here), about a week; the user's own prompts get it for free.

**What it costs.**
- One more install-time permission, AICore's `BIND_SERVICE` (normal; no prompt).
- Google's library reports usage to Google (device, app version, an installation id, sizes and timings; not the
  text) from a job in Booklight's process. **Checked on the Lenovo: with the uploader and its job removed from the
  manifest the model still answers, nothing is scheduled, and the network-state permission is not needed.**
  **Alex chose the library as shipped**: the reporting stays. `PRIVACY.md`, the privacy page and Play's data-safety
  form say what it reports; "no analytics" and "nothing in the background" are reworded to what is true.
- Google's terms for these APIs exclude apps "directed towards or likely to be accessed by" people under 18.
  Booklight's Play audience is 13 and over today; **it moves to 18 and over** (his choice; the Play session files it).
- Beta libraries; the model can be wrong, so its rows never run anything and never outrank a local match.
- About 1 MB more in the APK (estimate). The model itself is the system's, about 1.5 GB here, shared by all apps.

## 14. Motion

The motion spec is `docs/design/reviews-2.0/motion.md`: rows 21 to 55 in the format of the design system's table,
with how each is interrupted, what it does with animations off, and what would pop if built naively. It goes into
`design-system.md` with the build. What it settles, in short:

- Everything rides the existing springs (`place`, `lead`, `trail`, `pop`, `arm`). Two new named specs: `type`
  (Booklight typing an example) and `tick` (a countdown digit: 160 ms, 8 dp, not a spring).
- **Three moments get extra care:** the row that unfolds (the vertical counterpart of the panel's opening: contents
  laid out once and uncovered by one edge, closing the same way backwards); Booklight typing; and **the check that
  draws itself**, one 140 ms stroke wherever Booklight says "done" (a ticked task, the feedback word, "Your key
  works", "Copied" in a pin).
- The opening never changes: the tip arrives after it. The closing draws in to the field's centre line whatever the
  panel's height. The footer is anchored to the window's bottom edge. The window's width is never animated.
- A debug-only slow factor for every spring, so each row of the table can be stepped through on the device.
